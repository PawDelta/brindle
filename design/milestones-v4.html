The current goal panel, and what makes it good

This is the design as the app ships it today (GoalPanel.tsx in both modes, styles.css's .side-ms rows), drawn with the same goal every concept below uses: 40 milestones in 4 phases, 28 passed, 1 running, 1 with a failing check, 10 upcoming. The panel knows nothing of phases or failing checks yet, so it shows the failing one as In progress, as it would today. Every concept keeps the seven things below and changes as little else as it can.

keep · 1It is a list you can readEvery milestone, its number and its whole title, top to bottom, in the order the goal was written. Nothing is a hover or a click away.
keep · 2One line says it allThe 16 px ring and “28 of 40 milestones complete” in the sidebar; the bar and the same words in the panel. Glance, done.
keep · 3One vocabulary in both placesThe same 16 px circle: green with a tick once done, a hairline holding the number until then. The sidebar is the panel, tightened, not a second design.
keep · 4Colour is spent on state onlyGreen ticks, a purple dot for the phase, nothing else coloured. No boxes: hairlines and whitespace, like the rest of the app.
keep · 5The number is a handle“Milestone 29” is something you can say in chat, and the circle with a 29 in it is where you find it.
keep · 6The current milestone shows its checkIts command sits under its title in the sidebar, so you can see what brindle is verifying without opening anything.
keep · 7Calm words, calm footDone or In progress; “Checked 4 min ago” at the bottom. A failing check is brindle's job to fix, so the panel never shouts.
What it can't do yet. At 40, the 28 passed rows are as loud as the one that is running, the sidebar's list runs well past its pane (scroll it below), and phases don't exist, so “where are we” is a count, not a place. Each concept below answers that while keeping 1–7.

1 · thread · bold

The list becomes brindle's thread

The same rows, the same circles, but a thread runs through them, like the strands in the mark. A milestone that passed is a knot on it. A phase that has finished braids shut into one quiet row; the phase being worked on is the thread laid open, with its passed work stitched tightly along the line (one knot each, hover for the title, click to unfold) and only its open milestones keeping their words. A phase still to come is a loose, dashed strand. In the panel the progress bar becomes the thread laid flat: braided where a phase is done, knotted along the live phase, dashed ahead.

Keeps · the rows, the circle and tick, the count line, the number handle, the check under the running one, the foot.Adds · phases as places on one line; passed work compressed to stitches; the brand, quietly, in green only.At 40 · 2 braids + 1 open phase (8 stitches, 2 rows) + 1 strand = 7 rows tall.Trade-off · stitched titles are a hover away (the braid unfolds to a list on click).
Click a braid or a strand to open it; click the stitches to lay them out; hover a knot.
2 · contents · typographic

A table of contents

The goal was written as chapters, so it is set like a book's contents page: phases as chapters with roman numerals, milestones as entries with their arabic numbers, and dot leaders carrying every title across to its state in a right-hand column. The eye reads the left edge for where you are and the right edge for how it is going, and never has to search a row for its status. A finished chapter is one line, “✓ 10”. The live chapter lists its entries, with the passed ones folded into a single range line in the sidebar and written out in the panel. The circles go; the numbers, typeset, do their job.

Keeps · a readable list, number handles, the count line, the dot-and-word pill for state, calm words, the foot.Adds · chapters; a state column you can scan; leaders instead of alignment tricks.At 40 · sidebar: 4 chapter lines + 3 entries. Panel: all of chapter III written out.Trade-off · the tick circle is gone; running and failing read in the right column, not on the row's left.
Click a chapter to open or close it; click the range line to list the passed entries.
3 · folded list · closest to today

Today's list, with Outline's folding grafted on

Nothing about a row changes: the circle, the tick, the number, the title, the small mono line under the running one. What changes is that the rows now sit under phase folds, the part of v3's Outline that worked. A finished phase is one folded line with a green count; the phase being worked opens with all of its rows, passed ones included, exactly as they look today; a phase still to come is folded with its count. The panel is today's panel (label, pill, count, title, bar, “now:” line, the Milestones toggle) and its list, grouped the same way.

Keeps · everything; this is the current design with one new row type.Adds · a fold per phase with a count, and a tiny per-phase bar in the panel's fold line.At 40 · sidebar: 3 folds + the open phase's 10 rows = 13 rows.Trade-off · the open phase still lists its passed rows at full volume, so a phase of 20 is 20 rows.
Click any fold.
4 · today · bold

The state, written down

Above the list, brindle writes where things stand as two or three plain sentences, the way a colleague would say it if you asked: which phase, who is on what and for how long, what is failing and who has it, what comes next. The numbers in the prose are the same circles as in the list, so the sentence and the rows point at each other. Under it, only the rows that are moving right now; everything that has passed folds behind “Earlier” and everything to come behind “Later”, each with its count. The panel gives the sentences room and lists the moving rows in two columns, with the running check's live line.

Keeps · the rows for what matters now, the circles, the count line, the check under the running one, the foot.Adds · a digest that reads aloud; the timer and the failing check's age live inside it.At 40 · sidebar: 3 sentences + 2 rows + 2 folds, whatever the count.Trade-off · prose has to be written well by the engine; a long title makes a long sentence.
The sentences tick with the running check. Click Earlier or Later.
