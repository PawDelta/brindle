It would need
  • Taking the Library's head as a new revision here even though this copy changed too (today that happens only when it didn't).
  • Turning each section changed here since the last sync into a suggestion on their text. The before-and-after of two texts is already computed for a section's edit box.
  • A mark on those suggestions: their writer can't accept one that replaces a teammate's new wording, and the gate waits on them. Checked in the app; the Library would need the same check for it to bind.
  • The accepted supervisor suggestion in §4 never reached the Library, so it is filed again as yours. No Library change otherwise.
Against it
  • Whoever syncs second loses by default, even the doc's own author.
  • A long edit becomes a long suggestion that undoes all of theirs. It can't say "keep your half and mine".
  • Two suggestions accepted apart are two more rounds of sign-offs.
B

You reconcile it, section by section, and send one new revision.

The doc opens in a fourth mode, Reconciling. It shows only the sections that differ, each with both texts side by side. Sections only one of you changed are already picked for their writer. For the one you both changed, you pick a text or write one. Nothing is sent until every section has its text.

Drawn from the author's seat. A reviewer sees the same two columns with "Sign off this version" at the head of each, and can sign only one.

Who decidesThe required reviewers, by signing one whole version. The other writer is one of them here, so they must sign the winner too: they can't be outvoted, and you can't overrule them.
Your textComplete and untouched until one version wins. If theirs wins, yours closes and its differing sections are filed as suggestions on theirs.
Their textThe same, mirrored. Either writer can withdraw their own version at any time.
Sign-offs and threadsThe sign-offs are the decision, so there is no round afterwards: the winner already has them all. A thread shows on the version that holds its words.
The gateA state of its own, "choosing". It opens the moment one version has every required sign-off.
It would need
  • The Library holding two candidate revisions of one doc and taking sign-offs on either. Today only the head's count. This changes the shared-designs contract, the largest build of the four.
  • The gate counted per version, and a rule for a third edit: it lands on one version and voids that version's sign-offs.
  • The Differences view (the same side-by-side as B's, without the picks), here and wherever reviewers read the doc.
  • Filing the loser's differing sections as suggestions, as in A.
Against it
  • All or nothing. Reviewers can't take §4 from one and §5 from the other; priya@'s comment in the drawing is that complaint.
  • It can stall: split reviewers, or the other writer never signing yours.
  • Reviewers read twice, to settle what the two writers might have settled in a minute.

E

None of these, keep exploring

If A to D all answer the wrong question, say what was off and the next round starts from there. Directions that were left out on purpose, because each breaks one of today's facts:

  • Make it not happen. One person holds the pen at a time: while someone is in Editing, everyone else's edits are suggestions as they type. The conflict screen is then only for edits made offline.
  • Smaller revisions. If a revision were per section, edits to different sections would never clash, and only §1 here would need anyone. It changes what a sign-off covers.
  • Real merging. A three-way merge inside a section, with the clash marked in the text for a person to settle. It is the only way to keep both halves of that sentence without a supervisor, and it is new machinery.
  • Something else. A different owner for the decision (an org rule, the doc's first author always wins), or a different moment (warn before the second person starts editing).
