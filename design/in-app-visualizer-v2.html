1 · where visuals live

A session's visuals live in the session

When a decision is hard to read in a terminal, the agent publishes a visual: an HTML page that belongs to its session. It shows up as a link in the agent's chat and behind a small Visuals button in the session's header, which opens them beside the chat. You pick an option, Send, and the agent gets your answer as a message. There's no Visuals slot in the sidebar and no search: a session only ever has a handful.

A button, not a place“Visuals 2” in the session header counts the open ones; a branch dot means one is unread. It opens a panel beside the chat.
A short listOpen visuals are rows at the top of the panel. Decided ones fold into a single “N decided” line you can expand.
Gone with the session, unless keptThey're stored with the session and cleared when it's removed. Keep one and it stays, in Knowledge › Kept visuals.
A link and a calloutA new one prints a link in the chat and drops a small callout under the button. Neither takes focus or touches your draft.

3 · how a new visual reaches you

A link in the chat, and a small callout you click to open

The agent's chat always gets a link: you're usually reading it already, and it says what the visual is for in the agent's own words. On top of that, a small popup tells you it's there in case the chat has scrolled on. Two placements, side by side. Both are status, not dialogs: they never take focus, never move the caret, and nothing you type goes into them.

5 · lifetime

Stored with the session, cleared with it, unless you keep one

A visual is working material for a session, so it lives exactly as long as the session does. Pausing or finishing a session keeps them; removing it clears them. The few worth more than that, you keep.

1

Published

Stored under its session: a row keyed by the session id, files in .brindle/visuals/<session>/. Up to 20 revisions.

2

The session runs, pauses, finishes

Nothing changes. Reopen a paused or finished session and its visuals are there as you left them, fold and all.

3

The session is removed

Its visuals are deleted with its worktrees, and their Inbox cards close. The answers you sent stay in the agent's chat log.

Kept ones stay

Their last revision and your answer move to .brindle/kept/, read-only, in Knowledge › Kept visuals until you remove them there.

The last chance to keep one

Removing a session lists its visuals with a Keep box each, so nothing goes by surprise. Ones you already kept are ticked.

Remove “Ship the one-approval release flow”?

Its worktrees and its 5 visuals go with it. Tick any visual you want to keep.

Visuals

Where kept ones are later: Knowledge › Kept visuals

Beside the repo's decisions and docs, since a kept visual is usually the picture behind a decision. Each says where it came from and what you chose; ⌘K finds them under “Kept visuals”.

6 · planning docs for large requests

Before a big fan-out, the supervisor writes a plan and argues with it

Hand a supervisor something large and, before it starts any worker, it drafts a short planning doc: the goal, how it would split the work, the decisions that shape it with the alternatives it weighed and its own challenge to each, what's hard to undo, and what it doesn't know. It's a visual in the session, opened wide in the panel. You read it like a document, answer and comment where you have something to say, then approve or redirect. Workers start only after that. Small tasks never see one.

offered when the request is large

  • It would start three or more workers, or touch more than one repo
  • A step is hard to undo: a migration, a public API, a release, a deletion
  • The goal leaves real choices open (where state lives, what to cut)
  • You asked for one: “plan this first”

skipped and it goes straight out

  • One worker, a clear finish line
  • A bug with a reproducing test, a rename, docs
  • You said “just do it”, or the repo turns plans off
The supervisor offers, it doesn't insist. “Skip the plan” in the chat starts the work anyway; a plan is never more than a page.

7 · architecture sketch

One MCP tool, a store under the session, a sandboxed frame, a message back

Agents already reach brindle through its MCP server, and answers already reach agents as delivered messages. A visual adds one tool, one table keyed by session, one route for files and one for answers; the app adds a button, a panel and a frame. Keep adds a second, small store that doesn't belong to any session.

new piecesuntrusted: the agent's HTMLtrusted: brindle's own chromethe answer's path backKeep: the one way out of the session

The tool

publish_visual(
  title: str,
  html: str | path: str,      # one of; a file in the worktree
  session: str = "mine",     # default: the caller's root
  question: str | None,
  options: [{id, label, detail}] | None,
  replaces: str | None,       # new rev, same row
) -> {id, rev, link: "brindle://visual/v_8f2c"}

Also list_visuals(), for an agent to find its own session's; there's no listing across sessions. No polling for answers: they arrive as a delivered message, like a supervisor's. Agents can't keep, unkeep or delete: those are the person's.

Plans: propose_plan(markdown, decisions, split). brindle renders the markdown itself (trusted, no frame), so picks and comments anchor to its sections. While it waits, the session's assign calls queue behind it, as with depends_on; approve and redirect reuse approve_plan's approved / feedback shape, from the person to the supervisor.

Rendering

  • Served by the daemon on its own scheme, brindle-visual://, never from the app's origin, with a CSP header: default-src 'none'; script-src/style-src 'unsafe-inline'; img-src data: blob:.
  • <iframe sandbox="allow-scripts"> without allow-same-origin, forms, popups, modals or top navigation: an opaque origin with no storage.
  • CSP and the sandbox are not enough on their own: a sandboxed frame may still navigate itself (location.href = "https://…?d=…", a meta refresh, a link it clicks), and default-src doesn't govern that, nor WebRTC. So the host denies by default: the webview's navigation and request hooks (Tauri's protocol handler and on_navigation; WebView2 NavigationStarting / WebResourceRequested; WebKitGTK decide-policy) let a visual frame load only brindle-visual://<id>/r<n> and its own data: / blob: URLs. Every other navigation, subresource and redirect is refused, and the attempt marks the visual failed. RTCPeerConnection is removed before the page's script runs.
  • Proved before it's built on: a suite of hostile probe pages runs in each shipped webview against a local listener that must see zero requests, and stays as a CI test for the shell.
  • brindle prepends its token sheet and sends the theme on change, so a page written against the tokens matches the app in light and dark.
  • Only the open visual is mounted; the panel's other rows are just rows.

Answers and lifetime

  • The frame's only channel is postMessage({type:"choice", option}). The host checks the source window and that the id is one of the tool call's options, then highlights it. Nothing is sent until you click Send.
  • The engine records the answer, resolves the Inbox item and delivers Chose B · Gate on CI plus your note, quoted as yours. The row moves into the panel's decided fold.
  • Session-scoped: rows keyed by session id, files in .brindle/visuals/<session>/<id>/r<n>.html. Pause and finish keep them; remove deletes them in the same step as the worktrees, closing their Inbox cards. No time-based pruning.
  • Keep copies the last revision, your answer, and the session's name and repo to .brindle/kept/visuals/<id>.html and a kept_visuals row, so nothing points back at the session. Unkeep deletes the copy. Kept ones are read-only.
  • Arrival: the engine emits visual.published with the session; the app shows the callout only if that session is on screen with the panel shut, and holds it while a key went to the chat in the last 2 s.
